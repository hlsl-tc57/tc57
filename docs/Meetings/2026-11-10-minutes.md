## 6.2 [64-bit integer texture sampling](https://github.com/hlsl-tc57/tc57/issues/233)

**Deric Cheung** (Microsoft) presented a concern that DXC treats 64-bit integer elements differently dependent on whether the backend is DXIL or SPIR-V. **Justin Bogner** (Microsoft) suggested that the language standard for HLSL should either explicitly permit or explicitly forbid 64-bit integer sampling, instead of continuing on the approach of different behaviours.

**Chris Bieneman** presented the question of where is the line drawn in this type of operation between language standard and implementation-defined behaviour. **Jesse Barker** (Invited Expert, Unity) shared the perspective that as a user, implementation-defined behaviour here would be contextually expected.

Justin Bogner was supportive of providing for implementation-level discretion, but as an implementor explained that such loose specification made for challenging diagnostics and in particular assessing support for a variety of backends.

The committee reached consensus to leave this as an implementation-defined functionality, with a commitment to clearly specific the requirements for implementations to document or otherwise communicate supported data types.

## 6.3 [Variable template storage classes](https://github.com/llvm/llvm-project/pull/235273)

Chris Bieneman explained the problem space regarding variable templates behaving in a manner completely unexpected to a typical HLSL user, and not exactly as intended by the implementors or technical committee. Justin Bogner emphasized that C++ itself is under-defined in this area, and that HLSL will need to be more explicit to provide for desired behaviour, and might be in the position to file a defect with ISO-IEC/JTC 1/SC 22/WG 21, and hopefully bring C++ into alignment with the way forward that HLSL chooses.

## 6.4 [How should we define shader stages?](https://github.com/hlsl-tc57/tc57/issues/236)

Chris suggests that the committee focus on defining shaders which are most commonly used, or most clearly growing, and wait to define shaders which aren’t as likely to be used in the future unless there’s a demonstrated need for standardization. Several delegates within the committee—importantly, this was overwhelmingly users who might be the most impacted—expressed agreement with this approach.

Chris explicitly requested comments on his comment regarding guidance for implementations on this topic ([hlsl-tc57/tc57#236 comment-6747232839](https://github.com/hlsl-tc57/tc57/issues/236#issuecomment-6747232839)).

The conversation briefly included a subtle meta-question about the potential support for HLSL implementations on *devices*, such as “smart” TVs. The committee quickly reached consensus endorsing the linked comment, where an implementation is required to include a compute step.

The committee then discussed the rasterization stage. The committee reached consensus to refer to the appropriate stage as a *fragment* shader, as a more technically accurate description than “pixel” shading. Justin Bogner introduced a question for the committee to consider whether as a result, HLSL should explicitly alias “pixel” to “fragment”, and given the recent history of fragment shaders being occasionally referred to as pixel shaders without a specific pixel shader alternative, the committee reached consensus to do so.

The further definitions of shader stages were uncontroversial, and the committee agreed that they were satisfied with the outline, and that they would likely specify with more detail each stage in a future draft.

(see [follow-up comment](https://github.com/hlsl-tc57/tc57/issues/236#issuecomment-6774648540) for the ongoing conversation)
